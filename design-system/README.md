Retail POS is the till a small shop keeps running for a full trading day: touch-screen sales, a numeric keypad, receipt printing, stock and reports. It is not a marketing surface and not a kitchen-order screen — every rule below is written for someone standing at a counter, glancing at a screen at arm's length, often mid-conversation with a customer.

## Content fundamentals

Write for the person at the till, in the second person, in short imperative sentences: "Scan the barcode," "Select a payment method," not "The barcode should now be scanned." Screen titles and button labels are nouns or verb phrases in Title Case (`Sales`, `New sale`, `Void line`); body copy and helper text are sentence case. No exclamation marks, no "Oops!" — a till error is someone's money, keep it plain: "Barcode not found. Add it as a new product?" rather than "Uh-oh, we couldn't find that!" No emoji anywhere in the product. State amounts with the currency symbol and two decimals (`4,50 €` in `ca`/`es` locales, `€4.50` in `en`), set in `price` or `price-lg` so figures stay tabular.

## Colour

One accent, used deliberately: `brand` marks the single primary action on a screen (the sales screen's `Charge` button, a dialog's confirm button) and the active navigation item. It does not decorate cards, borders, or icons that aren't interactive — a screen with three orange elements has no primary action left. Semantic colour carries a fixed, narrow meaning and is never used for anything else: `success` for paid, completed, in stock; `warning` for low stock, a held ticket, a price override in effect; `danger` for void, refund, error, till closed; `info` for a passive notice such as a scan-to-import lookup. Every semantic colour is paired with its word or an icon (`discount-tag`, a check, a cross) — never colour alone, both because a busy floor is glanced at rather than read, and because `success` and `danger` must stay tellable apart under red–green colour vision. `success` is kept teal rather than a pure green for exactly that reason.

Build every screen from the surface scale, not from `ink` or `brand` as backgrounds: `surface-0` behind the whole window, `surface-100` for anything raised off it (the ticket panel, a dialog, a product tile), `surface-200` for anything sunken into it (the keypad's number well, a text input's fill, alternating table rows). `border` divides content that needs a visible line of its own — an input's outline, a table's rule — and is never decorative; `border-strong` is reserved for the focus ring and the selected state, so a till operator tabbing through a form can always find the cursor. Dark theme exists for the evening shift and unlit back rooms, not as a style option — it uses the same roles, never a black `surface-0` with the light theme's `brand` pasted on top.

## Typography

Manrope carries every UI surface: `display` for the two screens someone reads from across the counter (a full-screen "Till closed" state, the day-end total), `heading` for screen and dialog titles, `body-lg` for anything a cashier reads while also watching the customer — product names, ticket lines, button and keypad labels — and `body`/`caption`/`label` for the denser settings and reports screens where the operator is sitting, not standing. IBM Plex Mono is reserved for figures: `price-lg` for the running total, `price` for line items, change due and keypad entry, `price-sm` for unit prices and barcodes. Keeping money in a monospaced, tabular face means a ticket's digits never shift sideways as it grows and a total never looks like it's still loading. Never set body copy in the mono family, and never set a price in Manrope — the two families are how a glance tells "this is a word" from "this is a number" before it's even read.

## Spacing, radius and elevation

Everything sits on the 4px scale from `space-1` to `space-16`; a keypad key or any primary touch target is never shorter than `space-12` (48px) on its tap axis — this runs on a fixed till screen someone hits fast, not a mouse cursor. Radius scales with a surface's size: `radius-sm` for inputs and small chips, `radius-md` for buttons, keys and product tiles, `radius-lg` for cards, panels and dialogs, `radius-full` for badges and the logo mark's own punch-hole. Elevation is one faint step (`shadow-raised`) for anything lifted off `surface-0`, and one for anything floating above the screen (`shadow-overlay`); depth otherwise comes from the surface scale, never from stacking shadows or adding a second, heavier one for "more important."

## Iconography

Retail POS has no icon font and no third-party icon set: the six icons shipped in the **Icons** asset group (`cash-drawer`, `print`, `barcode-scan`, `card-payment`, `discount-tag`, `search`) establish the family a future icon earns membership in by matching, not by being close enough — 24×24, 2px stroke, rounded caps and joins, no fill except a single accent dot where the glyph needs one (`discount-tag`'s hole). Each is drawn in a fixed ink colour (`#241c14`) rather than `currentColor`, because these are served as plain images: recolour by exporting a themed copy, never by wrapping one in a coloured container. Use an icon beside its label the first time an action appears on a screen (a toolbar, a menu); once a cashier knows the sales screen, icon-only is fine for the same action repeated (a ticket line's void icon).

## Logo

The mark is a price tag: a rounded square with a punched string-hole, the one object every shop already has at the counter before Retail POS ever touches it. `retail-pos-mark.svg` is the light-background version in `brand`; `retail-pos-mark-on-dark.svg` swaps in the lighter dark-theme accent for a dark background — never place the light mark on a dark surface or vice-versa, the contrast is wrong both ways. `retail-pos-lockup.svg` / `retail-pos-lockup-on-dark.svg` add the wordmark, set in Manrope ExtraBold, for anywhere the app is named rather than just indicated (a login screen, a website header). `retail-pos-app-icon.svg` is the mark on a filled square for the window/taskbar icon, where a transparent background isn't an option. `retail-pos-ticket-logo.svg` is a separate, deliberately flat 1-bit black-on-white drawing — no accent colour, no soft edges — for the one output that can't render either: an ESC/POS thermal receipt printer, which dithers greys and drops anything under its resolution floor. Never scale the mark below 24px, never recolour it outside the two supplied variants, and never pair the mark with any wordmark but "Retail POS" in Manrope.

## What this system does not yet cover

This is a from-scratch system for a product still mid-modernisation, not a retrofit of the current Swing UI's Nimbus/Metal-era styling — [swing-development.md](swing-development.md) is the bridge from these tokens to FlatLaf, screen by screen, as the interface catches up. Component coverage here is deliberately small (`Button`, `NumericKey`, `ProductTile`, `StatusBadge`) — the pieces that recur across both the till and a future marketing/documentation site — rather than a full catalogue, since no such catalogue exists yet to draw from; add to it as real screens are rebuilt, matching an existing family before inventing a new one.
